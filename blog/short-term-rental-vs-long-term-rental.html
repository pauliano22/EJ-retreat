Short-Term Rental vs Long-Term Rental: Which Earns More?

September 21, 2026 6 min read STR Strategy

One of the biggest decisions a rental property owner faces is whether to list short-term or commit to a long-term tenant. The short-term rental vs long-term rental debate isn't just about income — it touches on management effort, tax strategy, market conditions, and your personal goals as an investor. Here's a data-backed breakdown to help you make the smartest choice for your property in 2026.

Revenue Potential: How Do the Numbers Actually Stack Up?

When comparing short-term rental vs long-term rental income, the headline numbers almost always favor STRs — but the full picture is more nuanced. According to recent STR market data, well-managed short-term rentals in popular US markets generate 1.5x to 3x the gross revenue of a comparable long-term lease. In high-demand vacation destinations, that multiplier can go even higher.

Why STRs Typically Win on Gross Revenue

Short-term rentals capitalize on peak pricing. Dynamic pricing tools allow hosts to charge premium nightly rates during holidays, local events, and high seasons — something a fixed monthly lease can never do. A property that rents for $2,000/month as a long-term rental might realistically earn $3,500–$5,500/month as a short-term rental at a healthy 65–75% occupancy rate, which is entirely achievable in most mid-to-large US metros and resort markets.

Of course, STR revenue is also subject to platform fees (typically 3–5% on Airbnb), cleaning costs, and higher utility bills. After accounting for operating expenses, the net advantage of short-term rentals is smaller — but still significant for most owners in the right markets.

When Long-Term Rentals Make Financial Sense

Long-term rentals win on predictability and simplicity. If your property sits in a market with weak tourist demand, strict STR regulations, or high vacancy risk, the stable monthly income of a long-term tenant can outperform a struggling short-term listing. LTRs also have lower turnover costs and zero platform dependency, which matters if you want truly passive income without active management.

Run Both Scenarios Before You Decide

Before committing to either strategy, pull market-level STR performance data for your zip code. Look at average daily rate (ADR), occupancy rate, and RevPAR for similar-sized properties. Many owners are surprised to find their "boring" suburban home outperforms nearby vacation properties thanks to corporate travel and relocation demand.

Management Burden, Risk, and Tax Implications

Revenue is only one side of the short-term rental vs long-term rental equation. The operational reality and tax treatment of each strategy are equally important — and often overlooked until it's too late.

Management Effort and Operational Risk

Short-term rentals require significantly more active involvement. Guest communication, cleaning coordination, maintenance requests, and dynamic pricing optimization are ongoing responsibilities. For owners who self-manage, this can feel like a part-time job. That's precisely why professional STR management companies like E&J Retreats exist — to handle every operational layer so owners capture STR revenue without the daily grind.

  • Guest Turnover: STRs have frequent check-ins and check-outs, requiring reliable cleaning teams and quality control systems after every stay.
  • Vacancy Risk: Unlike a signed annual lease, STR income can fluctuate month-to-month based on seasonality, local events, and listing rank.
  • Maintenance Wear: Higher guest volume means more frequent wear and tear, and issues must be resolved quickly to protect your ratings.
  • Regulatory Risk: Many US cities have passed or are considering STR licensing requirements, permit caps, or outright bans in certain zones.
  • Eviction Simplicity: LTR landlords dealing with non-paying tenants face lengthy, costly eviction processes — a risk STR owners largely avoid.

Tax Advantages That Often Favor STRs

The IRS treats short-term and long-term rentals differently, and understanding this distinction can meaningfully impact your after-tax returns. STRs (properties rented for an average of 7 days or fewer) can qualify as an active business under certain conditions, potentially unlocking the ability to use rental losses to offset other income — a significant advantage that long-term rentals don't typically offer. Additionally, STR owners may benefit from accelerated depreciation strategies like cost segregation, which can generate substantial paper losses in early ownership years. Always consult a qualified CPA who specializes in real estate to optimize your specific tax position.

Making the Right Choice for Your Property

The honest answer to the short-term rental vs long-term rental question is: it depends on your market, your property type, your risk tolerance, and your involvement level. In most US markets with reasonable STR demand, a professionally managed short-term rental will outperform a long-term lease on a net-income basis. But "professionally managed" is the key phrase. An underperforming, poorly optimized STR listing can easily lose to a reliable LTR tenant.
