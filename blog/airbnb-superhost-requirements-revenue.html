What Are the Airbnb Superhost Requirements?

Airbnb evaluates Superhost status automatically every quarter — in January, April, July, and October. To qualify, your account must meet all four criteria during the previous 12-month review period. There's no application process; Airbnb reviews your performance data and awards the badge if you hit every benchmark.

The Four Core Benchmarks

  • Minimum 10 completed stays (or 3 reservations totaling 100+ nights): This ensures Airbnb is evaluating hosts with enough booking history to make the data meaningful. New hosts need to ramp up activity before they're eligible.
  • 4.8 or higher overall rating: This is the most challenging requirement for most hosts. Even a handful of 4-star reviews can drag your average below the threshold, which means every single guest experience counts.
  • 90% response rate or higher: Airbnb measures the percentage of new messages you respond to within 24 hours. Pre-written quick replies and notification settings are essential to staying compliant here.
  • 1% cancellation rate or lower: You can cancel no more than 1 reservation per 100 bookings without losing status. Extenuating circumstances may be exempt, but host-initiated cancellations are closely tracked.

Pro Tip: Watch Your Rating Trend, Not Just Your Average

Your overall rating can look healthy at 4.82, but if your last 10 reviews average 4.6, you're heading toward a threshold problem. Monitor your rolling average regularly — don't wait for Airbnb's quarterly assessment to catch a downward trend.

The Real Revenue Impact of Superhost Status

Meeting the Airbnb Superhost requirements isn't just about prestige. The badge creates tangible financial advantages that compound over time. Studies and host data consistently show that Superhost properties earn significantly more than comparable non-Superhost listings in the same market.

How the Badge Affects Bookings and Pricing

The most direct impact is visibility. Airbnb's search algorithm actively favors Superhost listings, meaning your property appears higher in results even when guests aren't specifically filtering for Superhost status. Higher placement leads to more views, more inquiries, and more bookings — especially during competitive peak seasons.

Beyond search ranking, Superhost status signals trust. Guests — particularly first-time Airbnb users — gravitate toward the badge because it represents a vetted, reliable experience. That trust allows Superhost properties to command higher nightly rates without losing booking velocity. In many markets, Superhosts price 10–20% above comparable listings and still maintain strong occupancy.

Long-Term Compounding Effects

The revenue advantages of Airbnb Superhost requirements don't just show up in a single booking cycle. Superhosts benefit from repeat guests, more positive reviews (which further strengthen their ratings), and access to Airbnb's exclusive Superhost rewards program — including priority customer support, travel coupons, and bonus visibility during major promotional campaigns. Each quarter you maintain status, the competitive gap between your listing and non-Superhost competitors widens.

Common Mistakes That Cost Hosts Their Badge

Many hosts earn Superhost status and then lose it within a quarter or two because of avoidable operational gaps. The Airbnb Superhost requirements are straightforward, but maintaining them requires consistent systems — not sporadic effort.

The most common pitfalls include slow message responses during busy periods, a single bad review pulling the rating below 4.8, or one host-initiated cancellation that pushes the cancellation rate above the 1% threshold. Professional property management — or at minimum, rigorous personal systems — is the most reliable way to stay above every benchmark, every quarter.
