What to Look for in an Airbnb Property Management Company

Not all property managers are created equal. Some companies prioritize volume over quality, while others focus on long-term owner relationships and maximizing your revenue. Before signing any agreement, you need to dig into how a company actually operates — not just what their website says.

Fee Structures and Transparency

Management fees for an Airbnb property management company typically range from 15% to 35% of gross booking revenue, but the percentage alone doesn't tell the whole story. Some companies layer on additional charges that can quietly eat into your earnings. Always ask for a complete breakdown before committing.

  • Management fee: The base percentage of revenue charged for overseeing your listing, guest communication, and operations.
  • Onboarding fee: A one-time setup charge some companies collect to get your property listed and ready for guests.
  • Maintenance markup: Some managers add a percentage on top of contractor invoices — ask whether repairs are billed at cost or marked up.
  • Owner booking fees: Charges applied when you want to use your own property — a red flag if they're excessive.
  • Early termination fee: Penalties for ending your contract before a set period — make sure you understand the terms before signing.

Communication and Reporting Standards

A trustworthy property management company will keep you informed without you having to chase them down. Look for companies that offer a dedicated owner portal with real-time booking data, monthly financial statements, and a clear point of contact. Ask specifically: How quickly do they respond to owner inquiries? What's their after-hours protocol for guest emergencies? Vague answers to these questions are a warning sign.

Tip: Ask for a Sample Owner Report

Before hiring any Airbnb property management company, request a sample monthly owner statement. A professional company should be able to show you exactly how revenue, expenses, and net payouts are reported — clearly and with full line-item detail. If they hesitate or the report looks confusing, that's a red flag.

Evaluating Performance and Results

Promises are easy to make. What separates a great Airbnb property management company from an average one is a proven track record of results. When interviewing potential managers, push past the pitch and get into the data.

Key Performance Metrics to Request

Any credible management company should be able to share performance benchmarks for properties similar to yours — including average occupancy rates, revenue per available night (RevPAN), and average daily rate (ADR). If a company can't provide concrete numbers, or if their figures seem suspiciously high without context, keep looking. Companies like E&J Retreats, a national STR management company, use professional-grade market analytics to benchmark properties against real comparable listings — so owners know exactly what to expect before signing anything.

  • Occupancy rate: The percentage of available nights that are booked — a healthy rate typically falls between 60% and 80% depending on market.
  • Average daily rate (ADR): The average nightly price charged across all bookings, reflecting how well the company prices your property.
  • Revenue per available night (RevPAN): A combined metric that accounts for both pricing and occupancy — the truest measure of earning performance.
  • Review scores: Guest satisfaction scores on Airbnb and Vrbo reflect the quality of cleaning, communication, and overall guest experience.

References and Reputation

Ask for references from current owners — not just past clients. A confident, results-driven Airbnb property management company will have no hesitation connecting you with owners who can speak to their experience. Additionally, search the company on Google, the Better Business Bureau, and Trustpilot. A pattern of complaints around slow payouts, poor communication, or hidden fees should be taken seriously. E&J Retreats encourages every prospective owner to do their homework — because a company that's proud of its results will welcome the scrutiny.

Green Flags vs. Red Flags at a Glance

When you're comparing your options, keep this checklist in mind. The right partner will check most of these boxes without hesitation.

  • Green flag – Clear contract terms: Short lock-in periods, no excessive termination fees, and transparent fee schedules.
  • Green flag – Dynamic pricing tools: They use data-driven pricing software to maximize revenue across seasons and demand shifts.
  • Green flag – Dedicated owner support: A real person you can contact, not just a ticketing system.
  • Red flag – Vague fee structures: If they won't give you a complete list of potential charges upfront, expect surprises later.
  • Red flag – No owner portal: Without real-time access to your booking and revenue data, you're flying blind.
  • Red flag – Guaranteed income promises: Reputable managers share realistic projections — they don't guarantee specific income figures.
